// GAME DESIGN STUDY 01 - PLAYER ATTRIBUTES
GAME: LEGEND OF ZELDA BREATH OF THE WILD & TEARS OF THE KINGDOM

1. What attributes does the player have? Include major stats such as HP, stamina/mana, strength, defense, level, etc.

Link doesn't have traditional RPG stats like Strength = 40, Intelligence = 20, Dexterity = 35. Instead, much of his power is represented through smaller interconnected systems. The major attributes/systems include Hearts (health), maximum hearts, stamina, maximum stamina, weapon attack power, armor defense, equipment effects/buffs, temporary food/elixir effects, and battery/energy capacity in TOTK. Weapons also have their own attack values, shields have guard values, and armor pieces contribute defense and sometimes special effects. What's interesting from a design perspective is that Link himself doesn't have an obvious numerical "attack" stat. Much of his combat strength comes from what he's carrying and wearing.

2. Which are resources? Which values are consumed, depleted, restored, gained, or spent during gameplay?

A resource is something the player can consume, lose, restore, or manage. Hearts are depleted when Link takes damage and restored through food, sleeping, etc. Stamina is temporarily consumed by actions such as climbing, swimming, sprinting and certain combat actions, then regenerates. Weapons, bows, and shields effectively behave as resources because durability decreases through use until the equipment breaks. Food, ingredients, arrows and other inventory items are consumable resources as well. TOTK adds another especially important resource: Zonai Energy. Link's Energy Cell powers Zonai devices, meaning machines you've constructed can only operate while sufficient energy is available.

3. Which represent progression? What numbers communicate that the player's character is becoming stronger or advancing?

Maximum hearts and maximum stamina are two of the clearest representations of permanent character progression. As the player completes Shrines and spends the resulting rewards, Link can increase one of these capacities. That creates an interesting player decision: survivability versus mobility/endurance. Armor upgrades also represent progression. Upgrading armor increases defense and can make previously dangerous encounters much more manageable. Inventory capacity is another form of progression. Korok Seeds allow Link to carry additional weapons, bows, and shields. TOTK adds Energy Cell capacity as another progression system because Link can increase how long Zonai technology can operate. There's also a form of progression that isn't represented by Link's stats at all: player knowledge and skill. Early-game Link and late-game Link technically have many of the same basic abilities. But after dozens of hours, the player understands combat, physics, cooking, traversal, enemy behaviors and environmental interactions far better. That's an important game-design concept: player progression doesn't necessarily require character levels.

4. Which influence combat, and how? Don't just list them—briefly explain what they actually change.

Health/hearts determine how much damage Link can survive. Armor defense reduces the damage Link receives. Weapon attack values influence the damage Link deals. Weapon durability indirectly affects combat because powerful weapons can't necessarily be used indefinitely. The player has to decide whether an encounter is worth spending durability on. Armor and food effects can modify things like attack power, defense, elemental resistance, stealth and movement. Stamina can also matter during combat because certain actions consume it. TOTK makes equipment even more interesting through Fuse. Instead of simply finding a weapon with a predetermined attack value, the player can combine weapons with materials and monster parts, modifying their combat properties. So combat power isn't stored in one variable. It's produced by several systems interacting.

5. Design analysis: Why do you think the developers separated these into different attributes instead of giving the player a single Power stat?

Separate attributes allow designers to create trade-offs. A player can be strong in one area and weak in another, which creates different strategies and playstyles. BOTW and TOTK take that concept even further by putting a significant portion of Link's power into equipment, resources, environmental interaction, and player knowledge instead of traditional RPG character stats.
